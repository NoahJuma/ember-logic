# Physics Chapter 8: Momentum, impulse and collisions

Source: the user-supplied Young and Freedman, *University Physics with Modern Physics in SI Units*, 15th edition (2019), Chapter 8, printed pages 263–299. The complete book remains in the private library; it is not in this repository. The questions are bilingual adaptations of the source exercises, with source numbers, givens and subparts retained; worked solutions are original. Some small diagrams are excerpts from the supplied book and others are redrawn for clear language-neutral labels. Each has Swedish and English alternative text.

Mixed problems 8.63–8.105 connect the sections, include calculus, analysis and design; 8.106–8.109 are the archerfish passage. The bank also contains Q8.1–Q8.26, the chapter opening and bridging problems: 155 entries. Each offers independently accessible writing, hint, solution and video help; text is not automatically graded. Figures and the solution graph are local assets.

The quiz includes 72 numerical variants, 24 conceptual questions and four graph questions. It offers learning, topic, mixed-review and test modes with four levels. Its numeric tolerance follows the existing platform rule: the greater of 1% and 0.01 in the requested unit. The collision lab models a one-dimensional two-body impact, requires the left object to approach the right object and uses a restitution coefficient between 0 and 1. The separate 2D vector lab adds signed momenta rather than simulating an unconstrained two-dimensional collision. Both ask for a prediction before showing results. Values, answers, language and disclosures are saved locally where browser storage is available.

Source ambiguities are made explicit: VP8.14.4 needs an initially stationary center of mass to fix its location; 8.90 has an inconsistent first name, so the supplied 800 N is identified as an assumption for Nivaan; 8.94 needs a simultaneous-landing condition to infer the landing positions; and 8.99 gives insufficient data for the people's vertical impact energy. The idealized two-body neutron decay in 8.88 is labelled as the book's classical model.
